# Claims Procedure and Excess Rules

**Insurer:** FairQuote Insurance Limited
**Applies to:** All policy types
**Section Reference:** CLAIMS-01

## How to Report a Claim

All incidents must be reported to FairQuote Insurance as soon as reasonably possible, and in any case within **48 hours** of the incident occurring, even if you do not intend to make a claim. Late reporting may result in a claim being declined.

To report a claim, you will need:

1. Your policy number.
2. The date, time, and location of the incident.
3. Details of any other parties involved, including their names, contact details, and insurance information.
4. Photographs of the damage, where safe and possible to take them.
5. A crime reference number, if the incident involved theft, vandalism, or was otherwise reported to the police.

## Claims Process Timeline

| Step | Action | Typical Timeframe |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| 1 | Claim reported to FairQuote Insurance | Within 48 hours of incident |
| 2 | Claim acknowledged and claim reference issued | Within 1 working day |
| 3 | Vehicle inspected or engineer report requested | Within 5 working days |
| 4 | Repair authorised or total loss settlement offered | Within 10 working days of inspection |
| 5 | Repair completed at approved repairer, or settlement paid | Varies by repair complexity |

## Excess Explained

The excess is the amount you must contribute towards the cost of a claim. It is made up of two parts, both shown on your policy schedule:

- **Compulsory excess** — set by FairQuote Insurance based on your risk profile (for example, driver age, claims history, and vehicle group).
- **Voluntary excess** — an additional amount you chose to accept at the time of purchase in exchange for a reduced premium.

Your total excess is the compulsory excess plus the voluntary excess. This amount is deducted from any claim payment, or must be paid directly to the repairer before work begins.

## When Excess Does Not Apply

Excess is **not** payable when:

- The claim relates solely to injury or property damage caused by you to a third party (a third-party-only claim).
- Liability for the incident is accepted in full by another insurer, and they agree to reimburse FairQuote Insurance directly.
- The claim is for windscreen repair only (a separate, lower windscreen excess applies instead, shown on your policy schedule).

## Fault versus Non-Fault Claims

A claim is classified as **fault** if FairQuote Insurance cannot recover the full cost of the claim from another party. A claim is classified as **non-fault** if:

- Another driver has admitted liability, or
- Liability is clear from the circumstances (for example, you were hit while stationary), or
- FairQuote Insurance successfully recovers its costs from the at-fault party's insurer.

Non-fault claims, even where excess was paid, do not usually affect your No Claims Discount. Fault claims will typically reduce your No Claims Discount at renewal, unless No Claims Discount Protection applies.

## Total Loss Settlement

A vehicle is declared a total loss when the cost of repair exceeds a set percentage of its pre-accident market value, or when it cannot be safely or economically repaired. In this case, FairQuote Insurance will pay the market value of the vehicle immediately before the loss, less any applicable excess, as described in your policy's cover terms.
